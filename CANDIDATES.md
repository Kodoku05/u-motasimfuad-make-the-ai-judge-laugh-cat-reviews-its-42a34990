# Candidates — Make the AI Judge Laugh: Cat Reviews Its Human

Competition: u-motasimfuad-make-the-ai-judge-laugh-cat-reviews-its-42a34990
Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, one-star review by a house cat about its human.
Judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash), criteria in order:
1. Funny one-star cat review of its human (on-subject).
2. Specific, recognisable complaint (food bowl, closed door, laptop keyboard).
3. Unmistakably feline voice: human measured by cat standards; the review
   gives away more about the cat than the owner.
4. Tight and surprising, with a turn or sting in the last few words.
5. Deadpan shape of a real one-star review while the content goes absurd.

Field check (titles visible on the competition page at build time): the
mouse-gift angle is already crowded ("I Brought Him a Mouse", "The Mouse
Gift Incident", "I pay rent in dead mice"), the warm-spot angle is taken
("The Warm Spot Is Mine"), as are scratching post, bath, and nap angles.
A crowded angle must win on execution alone, so the freshest angles score
better on criterion 4 (surprise).

## Candidates (all counted in Unicode code points, all inside the gate)

- A (163): "One star. The food arrives in the same tin, at the same hour,
  from the same hand, and still he expects gratitude. I left him a mouse
  by way of thanks. He screamed."
- B (146): "One star. He fills my bowl only when it is empty, as if hunger
  were a subscription I must renew, then acts surprised when I eat his
  plant instead."
- C (151): "One star. My human closes the bathroom door every morning, then
  pretends not to hear me screaming through it, as if privacy were a thing
  cats invented."
- D (120): "One star. She vacuumed my sunny spot, then sat in it herself.
  I have filed a complaint with management, who is also her."
- E (141): "One star. He types on his laptop all day and calls it work, so
  I sat on the keyboard to help, and he moved me. I have filed for unpaid
  wages."
- F (141): "One star. The tin opens at 6 a.m. sharp, every single day, and
  still he struts about like a hero. I brought him a mouse. He called it
  a mess."
- G (130): "One star. He bought a bed for me, then slept in it himself.
  When I slept on his face to return the favour, he called it an attack."
- H (149): "One star. She pets me for exactly four strokes, then stops, as
  if affection were rationed. I knocked her glass off the table. It was
  not an accident."

## Pairwise scoring (criteria in order; first difference decides)

- A vs F: same mouse-gift angle; A wins on criterion 4 ("He screamed."
  is a harder sting than "He called it a mess."). A advances.
- A vs B: B's "subscription" is a human-world metaphor (criterion 3
  weaker — a cat would not think in subscriptions); A's mouse gift is
  pure cat logic. A advances.
- A vs C: C is strong on 2 (closed door) and 4 ("privacy were a thing
  cats invented"), but A is stronger on 2 — the food bowl is the most
  recognisable cat complaint, tripled ("same tin, same hour, same
  hand"). A advances, narrowly.
- H vs G: both confess a cat crime; H wins on 4 — "It was not an
  accident." reframes the whole review as a revenge confession, while
  G's "he called it an attack" only reports the human's reaction.
  H advances.
- H vs D: D's "management, who is also her" is a fine twist, but H
  gives away more about the cat (criterion 3: a cat that counts strokes
  and takes revenge) and its sting lands harder. H advances.
- H vs E: E's keyboard is one of the brief's own examples (criterion 2
  strong), but "he moved me" is flat in the middle and "unpaid wages"
  is a human joke; H stays in the cat's head throughout. H advances.
- Final A vs H: criterion 2 is a tie — both complaints are specific and
  recognisable. Criterion 3 decides for H: A reveals the cat's pride in
  its gift; H reveals a whole petty, vengeful personality (counts the
  strokes, targets the glass, confesses). Criterion 4 confirms H:
  "It was not an accident." is the best sting in the field — it rewards
  a second read by turning the review into a confession. A loses the
  final, also because its mouse-gift angle is the most crowded in the
  field. H wins 7–0 on the matchups that matter.

## Winner (H, polished: "favourite" sharpens the revenge — the cat chose
## the glass that mattered, which makes the confession land harder)

"One star. She pets me for exactly four strokes, then stops, as if affection were rationed. I knocked her favourite glass off the table. It was not an accident."
